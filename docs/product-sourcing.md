# Product Sourcing — What To Import From CJ, And Why

Written for the store's existing niche: **storage and display for trading cards**.
Read `store-runbook.md` first for the mechanics of importing and tagging.

---

## The strategic problem with the current catalog

All four current products are **one-time, high-consideration purchases** — display
boxes, a magnetic holder, a binder, a wall cabinet. They carry good margin and they
photograph well, but they share two weaknesses:

1. **They almost never repeat.** Nobody buys a second 36-slab cabinet next month.
2. **They are expensive to acquire a customer for.** High-consideration items need
   more ad touches per sale.

Meanwhile the actual best-selling accessories in this hobby are **consumables**.
Ultra Pro 3x4 toploaders rank as the single best-selling card accessory, and card
sleeves are described across the trade as the most essential accessory a collector
buys — they wear out, get used up, and are repurchased indefinitely.

**The fix is a two-layer catalog**, not a swap:

- **Display pieces stay the hero.** A wall of graded slabs is scroll-stopping
  creative and carries the average order value. These are what you advertise.
- **Consumables become the attach and repeat layer.** They raise cart value on
  every display order and bring people back without new ad spend.

Selling consumables *as the advertised product* is the trap — a $14 sleeve pack
cannot absorb the cost of acquiring a customer. Sell them as the second item in
the cart and the first reason to come back.

---

## Margin rule before any product decision

Dropshipping margin dies on ad cost, not product cost. Work backwards:

```
Retail price = CJ landed cost (product + shipping to customer) x 3.0
```

- **3.0x** is the floor for anything you intend to run paid traffic to. It leaves
  roughly a 66% gross margin, which survives ~30% ad-cost-of-revenue and still nets
  you something.
- **2.2–2.5x** is workable for organic, SEO and email traffic only.
- **Below 2x, do not import it.** One return or one chargeback erases the order.

Always use **landed** cost. A $6 item with $7 shipping is a $13 item.

For the CJ **Pricing Rule** setting: start at **3.0x with a round-to-.99 rule**.
Review anything whose landed cost exceeds ~$40 by hand — 3x on a $50 cabinet
produces a $150 price that the market may not carry, and the heavy items are
exactly where you should check against real competitor pricing.

## Tier 1 — Hero pieces (advertise these)

High order value, visually compelling, genuine margin room.

| Product | Target retail | Max landed cost | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall-mount slab display grid (10–20 slabs) | $60–120 | $20–40 | The wall-of-slabs photo is the best creative in this niche |
| Lockable UV display cabinet (36–80 slabs) | $80–160 | $27–53 | Highest AOV in the catalog; the "serious collector" purchase |
| LED-lit or rotating single-slab display | $35–60 | $12–20 | Lights and motion perform disproportionately well in video ads |
| Multi-slab tabletop display frame (6–12) | $40–70 | $13–23 | Bridges the gap between the $20 holder and the $80 cabinet |

You already stock two of these. The LED/rotating display and the mid-size frame
are the clearest gaps.

## Tier 2 — Attach items (the AOV layer, $10–30)

These are what a display-case buyer adds to the cart without thinking. Essential,
universal, and they ship light.

| Product | Target retail | Max landed cost |
|---|---|---|
| One-touch magnetic holders, multipack (35/55/75/100pt) | $15–25 | $5–8 |
| Acrylic slab stands / easels, 10-pack | $12–18 | $4–6 |
| Screwdown card holders | $12–20 | $4–7 |
| Slab storage box or brick (50–100 slab) | $25–40 | $8–13 |

**Stock one-touch holders in multiple point sizes.** 35pt fits a standard card,
100pt+ is needed for thick relic and patch cards, and getting this wrong is the
most common reason a card accessory gets returned.

## Tier 3 — Consumables (the repeat engine — bundle, never advertise alone)

| Product | Target retail | Max landed cost |
|---|---|---|
| Toploaders 3x4 35pt, 100ct | $12–18 | $4–6 |
| Penny sleeves, 1000ct | $12–20 | $4–7 |
| Matte card sleeves, 100ct (standard **and** Japanese size) | $10–16 | $3–5 |
| Team bags / resealable sleeves, 500ct | $10–15 | $3–5 |

**Only sell these in large multi-packs.** A 25-count toploader pack cannot clear
your shipping cost. 100ct and 1000ct counts also read as better value and are what
collectors actually buy.

Stock **Japanese-size sleeves** alongside standard. Japanese Pokémon cards are
slightly smaller, the sizes are not interchangeable, and most general stores only
carry standard — it is a cheap way to be the store that has the right thing.

## Tier 4 — Adjacent, worth testing

| Product | Target retail | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Deck boxes | $12–25 | Player market rather than collector; different audience |
| Custom playmats (print-on-demand) | $25–40 | POD means no inventory risk; strong margin |
| Grading submission kit (bundle) | $30–50 | Sleeves + toploaders + team bags + card savers, packaged as a kit |

The grading submission kit is the highest-leverage idea here: it is entirely made
of Tier 3 items you already stock, sells at a bundle price well above the sum of
its parts, and targets someone at the exact moment they are about to spend money
on grading.

## Bundles to build once the parts are in

Bundles are the cheapest AOV increase available — no new products, no new ad spend.

1. **"Grade & Display Starter Kit"** — acrylic display box + 100ct toploaders +
   1000ct penny sleeves. Targets the collector who just got slabs back from PSA.
2. **"Wall Display Starter"** — 5-pack magnetic holders + mounting hardware, sold
   as a set with a suggested grid layout.
3. **"Bulk Sort-Out Kit"** — storage box + team bags + penny sleeves. Targets the
   collector digging through a shoebox, which is a very large group.

## What not to import

- **Sealed cards, packs, singles, and mystery boxes.** Counterfeit exposure is
  severe, margins are thin, and the About page already commits publicly to not
  selling them. Breaking that promise is worse than the lost revenue.
- **Anything carrying licensed IP branding** — Pokémon, Bandai, Topps, Panini
  logos, or character art. This is the highest-risk category on CJ. Unlicensed
  goods draw IP takedowns that can put a payment processor hold on the whole store.
  Products *compatible with* Pokémon cards are fine; products *branded* Pokémon are
  not, unless the supplier can document a license.
  > The existing binder was imported with "Bandai" in its title. That branding has
  > been removed from the listing, and the Terms of Service page now carries an
  > explicit non-affiliation statement. Confirm the licensing position with CJ
  > before you advertise that product.
- **Anything claiming a UV rating the supplier will not state in writing.**
  Collectors buy specifically on that number.
- **Items over ~2kg or awkwardly shaped**, unless you have checked the real landed
  shipping cost. Large glass and wood cabinets are where dropship margin quietly
  disappears.
